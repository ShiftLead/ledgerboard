# TD-078: v3.0's ledger half — the last migration, a note for archived entries, and the removals

v2 still reads four old forms, only because old files hold them: a `Waits` field, a `Taken by` field, the old
decision form `decision (<who>) <text>` in `Blocked by`, and a `Resolved` header field. v3.0 stops reading them and
removes `migrate`. Before that can happen, every repo that takes v3.0 must hold none of them, open or archived, with
no hand edit. The design gives v2.8 the last `migrate` and a note for archived entries, and v3.0 the removals.
Since your Hold of 2026-11-14 (TD-099), v3.0 binds only ledgerboard, agent-cadence and ShiftLead. A roster repo is
untouched by v3.0 until it bumps its own pin, and how it gets there is designed later, in TD-100.

**The last migration (B2, in v2.8).** `migrate` gains four rules:
- `Taken by` becomes one plain Log line, `Taken by <value>`.
- `Waits` becomes one Log line on an archived entry. On an open entry it stops the run, since the wait needs a real
  gate (`until` or `event`) that someone chooses and `set` writes.
- A header `Resolved` moves to the body's `**Resolved:**` section. Where the body already has one, the run stops.
- The old decision form becomes `decision (<who>)`. Its text goes into an `**Ask:**` when the entry has none. When
  it has one, the existing Ask stands and the old item goes whole into one Log line. The run stops when `<who>` is
  neither a person nor a retired role, when an open entry has no Ask and the old item has no text to give it, and
  when the same `decision (<who>)` is already there.

The new Log lines are dated so that no gate's age moves. Under v2.8 the merge gate counts an old-form holder in the
base as a decision already there, so the rewrite does not read as gaining decisions. Once a repo has migrated, the
gate sweep sees decisions the old form hid, and may owe each a board item, which is right.

**A note for an archived entry (B1, in v2.8).** `ledger.py note TD-n "…"` takes an archived entry and writes it
where it is. It refuses one whose Status is outside the closed set. `set` and `edit` still take only open entries.

**The release order.** B1 and B2 have merged (TD-088, TD-089), and this repo's own ledger has been migrated with
them (B3, TD-090). v2.8 is tagged (2026-11-13, #281) and holds them. No v2.x is cut after it, so it is the last v2.x
minor. Then come TD-070's builds, then the removals (B4, TD-091), then v3.0, which is yours.

**What v3.0 removes (B4).** Each of the four forms, and a header `Location`, becomes a `retired` error on every
entry of a v2 ledger, open or archived, whether or not the edit touched it. On a v1 ledger a retired field is only
ℹ. The old decision form is also an unreadable item, so it is an error on an open entry even there, and ℹ on an
archived one. One helper with its own pattern finds the old form. `migrate` goes, with everything only it used.
v3.0's merge gate no longer counts an old-form holder in the base. So an old form rewritten as `decision (<who>)`
reads as a new decision, which needs a board item. The ledger's declared major stays 2.

**The precondition.** It binds the three repos that take v3.0 when it is cut: ledgerboard, agent-cadence and
ShiftLead. A repo is ready when, run with v2.8, `migrate --plan` shows no entry with changes or stops. Each consumer
runs it in its v3.0 vendor PR. Ledgerboard runs it before B4, with v2.8's own module. SPEC says the same without
naming repos: a ledger whose repo stays on an older release is untouched by v3.0 until that repo vendors it.

**A roster repo's way to v3.0 is TD-100's.** The roster repos are the repos other than these three that pin
agent-cadence. They skip their v2.7 remodel, and v3.0 leaves them as they are. When the first one is picked up
again, TD-100 designs how it reaches v3.0, from where ledgerboard and agent-cadence stand then. Rounds 9 to 15's
findings on such a remodel are its starting point. Target design §7.2 step 7 says only this.

**What B4 keeps of the revision.** The test is what v3.0 needs in the three repos whether or not a roster repo
exists:
- **Text 5′**, *Versions*' precondition sentence, cut to the precondition and the untouched ledger.
- **Texts 7's and 12's v1-ledger clauses** and their two fixtures. The three repos' ledgers are v2, but the module
  reads any ledger it is given, a roster repo's v1 one among them, and what it reports there must be defined.
- **Text 12's gained-decision clause** and its fixture: an old form rewritten on a stale branch in any repo is a
  gained decision under v3.0.
- **The old-form helper** and its two fixtures, since the old form can come back on a stale branch anywhere.
- **Smaller items kept:** a corrected fixture expectation (`gates.json`), text 8's wording, B4's run of the
  precondition on v2.8's tagged tree, and agent-cadence's roster-shaped fixture, since its tools still read roster
  repos v3.0 leaves untouched.

What served only a roster remodel goes: the procedure naming two agent-cadence pins, the two PRs it landed in, the
maintenance branch, the requirement on agent-cadence's `bump`, and the sentence telling a roster repo to run the
last `migrate` first.

**Why the cut.** The first pin came back in four rounds, and each fix added machinery for repos nobody is working,
to run at an unknown date by which agent-cadence's pins will have moved. A procedure designed when the first roster
repo resumes starts from what is true then.

**What the consumers do.** These are lines for their own entries, filed through each repo's question seat.
- **agent-cadence:** before its ledger migrates, an id that is a real wait moves out of the old form's text into
  `Blocked by`. Its v3.0 vendor PR removes its `migrate` command and its date helpers, the `accept_taken_by` keyword
  and the `old_form` read. Its tests read a roster-shaped fixture without an exception, since its tools still read
  roster repos that v3.0 leaves untouched.
- **ShiftLead:** its Queue card's *held until …* reads the `**Ask:**` before its ledger migrates. It vendors the
  minor and runs `migrate` in separate PRs.

**Left out.** The board's half of v3.0 (TD-070, TD-095). A roster repo's way to v3.0 (TD-100). When v3.0 is cut.
Renaming `**Resolved:**`. Removing the Status note lift.

**The rounds.** Rounds 1 to 8 designed the plan, and you said Go on 2026-11-12. The revision (TD-096) ran rounds 9
to 15 on the roster remodel, and you cut it with a Hold. The cut (TD-099) ran one round, 16. Its fact-check raised
four minor findings and its design review five, none build-changing, so the rounds ended by the stop rule. The closing fact-check raised five more
minor ones, applied. One
finding was rejected, F228, since TD-099 and TD-100 share an id take because they were reserved together. The design
review judged the cut the right shape.

You said **Go** on 2026-11-14. TD-091 (B4) now names what it keeps, as listed above, and no longer waits on TD-096.
