# TD-132: an answer a delegate makes for the person

ShiftLead's director seat is to answer some board questions on your behalf, starting with merges inside your merge
line. Today a board answer records only who answered, and every check accepts an answer only from the person a
hold waits on (or, on a board where anyone listed may answer, from anyone on its list of people). So a seat
answering for you can either write your name, and the record says you decided something you did not, or write its
own, which every check refuses. Putting the seat on the list of people would make it a person, which it is not.

**The record.** A delegated answer names three things: who wrote it, whom it counts for, and under which authority,
for example *decided 2026-11-20 by director for pat (merge-line): Merge*. The ledger's line for a lifted hold says
the same. Every check that asks whose answer this is reads it as yours, so holds lift and the question leaves your
queue as if you had pressed it; only the new checks read the delegate. A tool that shows who answered can show the
delegate beside you. Old-style boards, which nothing writes any more, are not extended.

**Where you grant it.** A board gains one line beside its list of people, for example *Delegates: director for pat
(merge-line, design-go-routine)*: each grant names the delegate, the person it acts for, and the kinds of decision
it may take. There is no catch-all kind. A delegate may not be on the list of people, may not be granted for
itself, and may act only for someone on that list. A line with any mistake in it grants nothing at all until it is
fixed, though your own answers keep working. So removing a person from the list of people stops every delegate's
answers on that board, for everyone, until the grant line is fixed, and the only sign is a note in the board check;
this is chosen, so that a line that no longer reads cleanly grants nothing rather than a part someone must pick out. Changing this line is never something a lift's quick path accepts:
who may change it is the consumer's rule for your own files, and agent-cadence makes the board's main file one of
those, checking the line exactly.

**What it may answer.** Whoever raises a question marks what kind of decision it is (its scope, such as
*merge-line*). A delegate may answer only a question that carries a scope its grant names, that you yourself could
answer, and whose hold the answer can carry through in the same change: one entry and one hold, or no hold at all.
It may give any of the question's answers, including *Not yet*; a grant is for a kind of decision, not for some of
its answers. It may not answer a question about another repo's entry in this release, since that repo could not
check the grant. A question the gate sweep asks again carries no scope, so only you answer it.

**How the checks hold the line.** The board writer refuses a delegated answer the grant does not cover and says
which rule failed. The merge check judges the grant as it stood before the change, so one change can never grant
itself the authority it then uses, or rescope the question it answers. A delegated answer always writes the
ledger's side of it in the same change, so no delegated answer is ever left owing a lift that someone could later
record in your name. The quick path for lifting a hold on the ledger alone accepts no delegated trace at all: any
such lift goes to review. One narrow gap is stated rather than closed: if a reviewer merges a delegated answer
without its ledger side, which the writer cannot produce and the check refuses, a later lift in your name could
pass; the gate sweep then files a step that names the delegate and tells the session to write the lift in the
delegated form, never as your own override. Where both you and a delegate answered the same hold, the step names
the delegate: that lift goes to review, which sees both answers, whereas an override in your name is never reviewed.

**What it cannot check.** The format compares names; it cannot tell who raised a question. A delegate that could
raise its own question, scope it and answer it, or that leaves the delegate out and writes your name, is caught
only by the consumer: agent-cadence's quick path for board edits must not let a delegate add a scope, and the seat
must always write the delegated form.

**The release.** A minor release, v3.4: spec, code and test cases in one change here. No delegate writes an answer,
and no question carries a scope, until every repo that reads the board has the new version, since an older reader
would see the answer as missing and put the question back in your queue. Then agent-cadence's update (its board
tool passes the delegate and scope, and its rules guard the grant line), then ShiftLead's, after which you write
each board's grant line and the director seat starts answering.

**Options set aside.** Putting the seat on the list of people; per-answer grants (*Merge* but not *Hold*); a
catch-all kind; a delegate answering a question raised already answered; and delegation on another repo's entries.

**The rounds.** Six review rounds, the limit for a change to a shared format, found problems that would change the
build in every round: 7, 8, 2, 2, 2 and 2. One topic recurred in every round: how a hold is lifted on the ledger
alone after a delegated answer. After round 4 the design changed shape so that a delegated answer carries its own
lift, and rounds 5 and 6 found only holes and details in that shape. You bought a seventh round. Its reviewer read
round 6's fixes, tried the recurring topic on every kind of question it could build, found it holding on each, and
judged the shape right. It found one problem that would change the build, on another topic: a sentence of the spec
text left over from an earlier draft refused a question with no hold that the design accepts. It also found six
small points; all are fixed. The closing check of round 7's own fixes then found two statements, left stale by
those fixes, about which refusal a malformed question meets first in the merge check. Either way the change goes
to review, so neither changes what passes. It also found four small points, three fixed and one set aside. No design
review has read these fixes. The recommendation is One more round, so a reviewer reads them before anything is built.
