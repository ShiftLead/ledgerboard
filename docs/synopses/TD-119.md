# TD-119: the gate sweep offers the entry's own answers

When a ledger entry waits on a person and no board question asks about it, the gate sweep (the periodic job that
notices stuck holds) files a question for them. Today that question always offers **Go** or **Not yet**, whatever
the entry actually asks. ShiftLead's sweep on 2026-11-15 filed two such questions on this board: one entry asked
*one more round, or go ahead now?*, the other asked for *Works* or *Not right*. You could not press the answer the
question asked for, and typing *Works* would not have lifted the hold, since only Go does.

**Where an entry's answers live.** An entry may carry its own answers in an `Answers:` section of its body, written
exactly like a board question's answers (each with what happens next, one marked as the default), and an optional
`Lifts:` section naming which of them lift the hold. With no `Lifts:`, only Go lifts, as on the board. ShiftLead
already writes such an `Answers:` section on one entry. Answers are not read out of the Ask's prose, which would be
guessing, and not kept in the entry's header, which holds one-line facts.

**What the sweep does.** When it files a question for an entry that has usable answers, it offers them, with its
recommendation naming which ones lift and, where *Not yet* is offered, that it keeps waiting. Otherwise it offers Go or Not yet as today, and, where the entry had answers
it could not use, says so. It never fails over an entry's answers. A question already on the board is never
rewritten when its entry's answers change.

**Which answers can be used.** A new reader decides, the same way for the sweep, the ledger check and a writer. Each
section must be one paragraph, starting on a line of its own after a blank line. The reader refuses an `Answers:`
or `Lifts:` line stuck to the line above it (so never read as part of the question or of an answer), a second
paragraph in either section, a wrapped answer whose next line starts in bold, an entry the ledger cannot read, two
sections of one name, an empty `Answers:` or `Lifts:`, answers the board's own writer would refuse (fewer than
two, two alike, a lift naming an answer not offered, a form like *Hold: <note>* named in `Lifts:` by anything but
its whole label), two marked defaults, a `Lifts:` with no `Answers:`, and answers of which none would lift the
hold. Bold words inside an answer's line are fine, and so is a field named `Answers` in the entry's header lines, which
stays the repo's own. (A *form* is an answer with a blank to fill, like *Hold: <note>*; a *wrapped* answer is one
long enough to run onto a second line.)

**The ledger check.** It flags an entry waiting on a person whose answers cannot be used, and the ledger writer
refuses an edit that makes that fault. An entry already carrying one can still take unrelated edits; reopening or renumbering it is refused, since that
makes the fault anew. An entry
with no hold is never flagged, so a repo's own free-form answers there stay legal. An entry with a hold that
already carries its own `Answers:` section (ShiftLead has one) is offered those answers from the release on, and
flagged if they cannot be used.

One set of answers serves every person the entry waits on, and an entry asked about through its parent's
question (as one of its parts) is asked with the parent's answers, not its own.

**What an answer does on the board.** A lifting answer lifts the hold. *Not yet*, where offered, keeps waiting, as
today. Any other answer, such as *One more round* or *Not right: …*, is an instruction to the entry's session: the
question stays open, so the sweep asks nothing while the session works. The session closes it when done and asks
again itself if the work ends in a question; for the sweep's re-ask window after your answer (30 days by
default) it files nothing of its own. An
entry that offers no *Not yet* gives you no way to defer, only to lift or instruct.

**Plain words.** Each answer must say who acts, checked by the tool in agent-cadence or ShiftLead that writes the
section, as any answer you read is.

**Release.** A minor release, v3.3; v3.2 was tagged today, so the build waits on nothing. Neither agent-cadence
nor ShiftLead needs a code change: both pass the sweep's answers through. On your go a session tells ShiftLead it
can write answer sections on the two entries the sweep asked about, and how to have those two questions asked
again.

**Options set aside.** Keeping Go or Not yet and asking you to type the answer (a typed answer lifts nothing);
reading the answers out of the Ask's wording; a header field; answers inside the Ask itself, whose first paragraph
is its question.

**The rounds.** Two rounds, then a third you bought. The first design review found two problems that would change
the build, the second none, and the closing check one. Round 3's checks found three more: example answers that
the writers would refuse, a vague rule for spotting a stuck-on section line, and a release note the two v3.3
builds would have shared and contradicted (each now writes its own). Its design review found three: an empty
`Lifts:` read two ways, test cases filed where they could not show what they claim, and an `Answers:` line stuck
to the question being missed entirely. The closing check found one more: the new rule would also have refused a
repo's own `Answers` field in the entry's header lines, so it now looks at the body alone. One topic keeps coming back: where the answer sections start and end. Each
fix closed one way of misreading text stuck to them and left another. The reviewer judged the design itself sound
and the boundary rule the weak point; round 3 replaces the patches with two rules over the whole entry (any
stuck-on `Answers:` or `Lifts:` line is refused, and each section is one paragraph). If another round still finds a
hole there, the fallback is to make each section a single line. No design reviewer has read round 3's fixes, so the
recommendation is one more round.
