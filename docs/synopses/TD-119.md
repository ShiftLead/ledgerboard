# TD-119: the gate sweep offers the entry's own answers

When a ledger entry waits on a person and no board question asks about it, the gate sweep (the periodic job that
notices stuck holds) files a question for them. Today that question always offers **Go** or **Not yet**, whatever
the entry actually asks. ShiftLead's sweep on 2026-11-15 filed two such questions on this board: one entry asked
*one more round, or go ahead now?*, the other asked for *Works* or *Not right*. You could not press the answer the
question asked for, and typing *Works* would not have lifted the hold, since only Go does.

**Where an entry's answers live.** An entry may carry its own answers on one line of its body, starting
`Answers:`, written exactly like a board question's answers (each with what happens next, one marked as the
default), and an optional separate line (after a blank line) starting `Lifts:` naming which of them lift the hold. With no `Lifts:`, only
Go lifts, as on the board. Each is a single line, as your hold asked: nothing after the line is ever read as part
of it. ShiftLead wrote such an `Answers:` line on one entry, since archived, so no open entry has one today. Answers are not read out of the Ask's prose,
which would be guessing, and not kept in the entry's header lines, which hold short facts.

**What the sweep does.** When it files a question for an entry that has usable answers, it offers them, with its
recommendation naming which ones lift and, where *Not yet* is offered, that it keeps waiting. Otherwise it offers
Go or Not yet as today, and, where the entry had answers it could not use, says so. It never fails over an entry's
answers. A question already on the board is never rewritten when its entry's answers change.

**Which answers can be used.** A new reader decides, the same way for the sweep, the ledger check and a writer. It
refuses three kinds of misplaced text, so none is ever misread quietly:
- anything after the line in the same section, such as an answer wrapped onto a second line, a line stuck on
  below, or a stray sentence after it;
- an `Answers:` or `Lifts:` line stuck to the line above it, or indented, so it is not read as part of the
  question;
- an `Answers:` or `Lifts:` written inside the other line, such as `Lifts:` typed at the end of the answers' line,
  even where a stray pair of asterisks earlier in the line would otherwise hide it.

It also refuses an unreadable entry that has an `Answers:` or `Lifts:` line, two lines of one name, an empty `Answers:` or `Lifts:`, answers
the board's own writer would refuse (fewer than two, two alike, including two that differ only in capitals such as *Go* and *go*, a lift naming an answer not offered, a form like
*Hold: <note>* named in `Lifts:` by anything but its whole label), two marked defaults, a `Lifts:` with no
`Answers:`, and answers of which none would lift the hold; that refusal lists the labels it read, so an answer written in bold, which reads as a different label from *Go*, shows why. Text inside a code block is ignored, but a code block
that is never closed hides nothing: a real `Answers:` line after it is still caught. Other bold words inside an
answer are fine. A few look-alikes are plain text, read by nothing and refused by nothing, so the entry gets Go or
Not yet as today: an `Answers:` written as a list item or a quote, after other words on its line, spelled
`Answer:`, or among the header lines. (A *form* is an answer with a blank to fill, like *Hold: <note>*.)

**How this fits TD-124.** TD-124 is a separate design for where every section of an entry starts and ends, which
you asked for the same day as this hold. This design does not patch that rule again. The three refusals above stay this design's own
after TD-124 lands: TD-124's general check also flags the plainest stuck-on line, but not the indented or
oddly written ones, so handing the refusal over would let those through quietly. TD-124 catches the look-alikes
above and two shapes hidden in comments, and whichever of the two builds lands second has this reader use TD-124's
shared line reader, fed text with comments removed so it reads exactly as here. TD-124's later strict release
retires some older ways of writing a section's name, which this design reads until then; after it, an `Answers:`
written the old way is refused, never silently read as plain text with Go or Not yet offered instead. TD-124's
build entries say both, so their builders know.

**The ledger check.** It flags an entry waiting on a person whose answers cannot be used, and the ledger writer
refuses an edit that makes that fault. The check names the first fault it finds and says so, since fixing that one
may show another; fixing both in one edit always works. An entry already carrying one can still take unrelated edits; reopening or
renumbering it is refused, since that makes the fault anew. An entry with no hold is never flagged, so a repo's
own free-form answers there stay legal. An entry with a hold that already carries its own `Answers:` is offered
those answers from the release on, and flagged if they cannot be used; no open entry has one today.

One set of answers serves every person the entry waits on, and an entry asked about through its parent's
question (as one of its parts) is asked with the parent's answers, not its own.

**What an answer does on the board.** A lifting answer lifts the hold. *Not yet*, where offered, keeps waiting, as
today. Any other answer, such as *One more round* or *Not right: …*, is an instruction to the entry's session: the
question stays open, so the sweep asks nothing while the session works. The session closes it when done and asks
again itself if the work ends in a question; for the sweep's re-ask window after your answer (30 days by default)
it files nothing of its own. An entry that offers no *Not yet* gives you no way to defer, only to lift or instruct.

**Plain words.** Each answer must say who acts, checked by the tool in agent-cadence or ShiftLead that writes the
line, as any answer you read is.

**Release.** A minor release, v3.3, not yet tagged, possibly shared with the merge-gate rule already built for
TD-115 and the first build of TD-124, so the build waits on nothing; each writes its own release note. Neither agent-cadence nor ShiftLead needs a code
change: both pass the sweep's answers through. On your go a session tells ShiftLead it can write answer lines on
its entry the sweep asked about that is still open (the other has been archived), and how to have its question
asked again. Questions already on the board stay as filed, with Go or Not yet, and you may answer them as they
stand. Go files one build.

**Options set aside.** The multi-paragraph sections of the first three drafts, whose edges every round found a new
way to misread; keeping Go or Not yet and asking you to type the answer (a typed answer lifts nothing); reading the
answers out of the Ask's wording; a header field; answers inside the Ask itself, whose first paragraph is its
question.

**The rounds.** Three rounds, then your hold, then three rounds on the one-line revision, the last two ones you
bought. Problems that would change the build, per design review, rounds 1 to 6: 2, 0, 3, 2, 2, 0. The single line
settles where the answers end. Round 4 closed two holes at the edges (a code block never closed, a `Lifts:` typed on
the answers' line). Round 5 kept the stuck-on-line refusal here rather than handing it to TD-124, and made the
search for a `Lifts:` inside the answers' line try every pair of asterisks. Round 6, the last the limit allows, read
those fixes, judged the design worth building as it stands, and found nothing that would change the build, so the
rounds end. Its nine small fixes and the closing fact-check's seven are applied: the archived ShiftLead entries,
the reader fed text without comments when it moves onto TD-124's line reader, the old spellings refused after
TD-124's strict release, the exact recommendation wording, the two-labels-in-capitals refusal, the check naming its
first fault, where the new release-note sentence goes, where the header lines end, and the release shared with
TD-124's first build. No reviewer has read these small fixes; none changes what is built.
